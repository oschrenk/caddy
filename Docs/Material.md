# Sheet Goods at San Miguel

- prices are in quetzales (Q) and are the shelf price for a whole sheet
- San Miguel does not offer a cut-to-size charge online, but in person
- Two sheet sizes dominate: **4' × 8'** (1220 × 2440 mm, the standard) and **6' × 8'** (1830 × 2440 mm, used for melamine and some MDF so cabinet makers get fewer seams in a wardrobe). SKUs follow a family prefix — `T5-` is plywood, `T4-` is MDF, `T6-` is melamine, and so on — which is the fastest way to tell two similar listings apart.

## Quick decision table

| If you're building… | Buy | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Painted indoor carcass, shelves, a shoe cabinet body | **MDF 1/2" or 3/4" 4'×8'** (Q175 / Q271) | Dead-flat faces take paint with no grain telegraphing. Cheapest good paint surface in the shop. |
| The same carcass, but it must be light and take screws in the panel edge | **Plywood sangre 1/2"** (Q199) | Roughly half the weight of MDF and edge screws actually hold. You pay for it in filling and sanding. |
| A rolling cart that carries load and gets knocked | **Plywood sangre 1/2"–3/4"** (Q199 / Q316.50) | Cross-banded veneer resists the racking and impact that snap an MDF panel. |
| Frames for woven-rattan front panels | **Tablero pino listonado 5/8"** (Q699/sheet) or ripped **plywood sangre 3/4"** | Rattan cane needs a groove or a rebate held by continuous long-grain. MDF frames this narrow break at the corners. |
| Cabinet back, hidden | **Respaldo 3 mm** (Q97) or **MDF 3 mm** (Q62) | Both are 4'×8' at under Q100. Respaldo comes pre-finished on one face, MDF does not. |
| Anything that will get wet or live outdoors | **Plywood fenólico pino** (Q133–Q580.50) | Phenolic glue line does not delaminate when soaked. Nothing else here survives water. |
| A finished cabinet with no painting at all | **Melamina 5/8" 6'×8'** (from Q329) | Arrives finished on both faces. You never open a paint can. |
| Concrete formwork | **Plywood fenólico black film 1/2"** (Q291) | The film releases from cured concrete and survives multiple pours. |

For the two projects on hand: the **shoe cabinet** carcass is an MDF job (3/4" for the sides and top, 3 mm respaldo or MDF for the back) with **solid pine** for the rattan frames. The **rolling cart** is a plywood sangre job in 1/2" — matching the 12 mm already specified in `Caddy/Cutlist.md`.

- Wood chips pressed with resin into a slab. No grain, no veneer, no face treatment.
- This is the cheapest square metre in the shop and the weakest.

**Typical use:**
- disposable shelving, drawer bottoms, jigs, shop fixtures

**Pros**
- cheap
- cuts fast and does not dull blades the way MDF does.

**Cons**
- texture shows through paint no matter how much primer you use
- the face is not paintable to a smooth finish without skimming it first.
- Screws driven into the edge split the chip matrix and pull straight out under load.
- shelves longer than about 600 mm sags permanently under books.
- Absorbs water and swells irreversibly.

### Enchapado — Veneer-faced plywood

[View on sanmiguelmadera.com][sm-enchapados]

<img src="https://www.sanmiguelmadera.com/web/image/product.template/89463/image_1024" alt="Mahogany-veneered plywood face" width="240">


- *Enchapado* means "veneered". One species only: **caoba** (mahogany), Macosa brand.
- The grade suffix is the important part:
  - **1C** = *una cara*, one good face. The other is a utility veneer.
  - **2C** = *dos caras*, both faces mahogany. Use when both sides show.

**Typical use:**
- furniture that will be stained or clear-finished rather than painted
- door panels
- visible mahogany without paying for solid stock

**Pros**
- mahogany colour and figure at roughly a quarter the cost of solid mahogany
- sands and clear-finishes to a genuinely good surface with no filling
- 2C at Q317 for 1/2" is the closest thing here to a cabinet-grade panel

**Cons**
- face veneer is thin — one aggressive pass with 80-grit sands through to the core, unrepairable
- pointless if you are painting; you pay a premium for a face you cover
- one species, one brand, so no choice on colour or figure
- core is standard utility ply, so the edge still shows voids and needs banding or a solid lipping

### MDF — Medium-density fibreboard

[View on sanmiguelmadera.com][sm-mdf]

<img src="https://www.sanmiguelmadera.com/web/image/product.template/87082/image_1024" alt="MDF sheet, showing the smooth homogeneous face" width="240">


- Wood fibre broken down finer than particle board and pressed into a completely homogeneous slab. No grain, no voids, no core layers.
- House stock is **Trupán** from Chile. There is also a white-faced version under the SM brand.

**Typical use:**
- painted furniture and painted cabinet doors
- shaped and routed edge profiles, drawer fronts
- templates

**Pros**
- best paint substrate in the shop — perfectly flat, no grain to raise or telegraph
- machined edges hold a crisp routed profile, which plywood and particle board cannot
- no voids, so a cut edge is solid and can be filled and painted rather than banded
- cheap for the finish quality: 1/2" Q175, 3/4" Q271

**Cons**
- heavy — a 3/4" sheet is around 45 kg, awkward alone, and it matters for a rolling cart or anything wall-hung
- edge screws strip out unless you pilot precisely and use a coarse confirmat-style screw
- cut edge is porous and drinks paint; seal it (thinned PVA, shellac, or two primer coats sanded back) or it stays fuzzy
- swells like a sponge on contact with water and never recovers — not for bathrooms or near a mopped floor
- very fine resin-laden dust when cut. Wear the N95.

### Melamina — Melamine-faced board

[View on sanmiguelmadera.com][sm-melamina]

<img src="https://www.sanmiguelmadera.com/web/image/product.template/87108/image_1024" alt="Melamine-faced board in a woodgrain decor" width="240">


- Particle-board or MDF core with melamine-impregnated decor paper fused to both faces under heat and pressure. Arrives finished.
- By far the largest subcategory — roughly 73 SKUs, almost all **5/8" in 6'×8'**, differing only in decor.

**Typical use:**
- kitchen and wardrobe carcasses
- office furniture, shelving
- anything where the whole point is to skip finishing

**Pros**
- zero finishing work — cut, edge with tapacanto, assemble, done
- the face is genuinely hard-wearing and wipes clean, better than most paint you would apply yourself
- 6'×8' means a full-height wardrobe side comes out of one piece
- huge decor range: white, black, greys, dozens of wood prints, super-matte, stone looks

**Cons**
- chips badly on the exit side of every cut unless you score first or use a fine-tooth blade
- a chip in melamine cannot be repaired, only hidden
- every exposed edge needs tapacanto or it looks unfinished and the core absorbs moisture
- the face takes neither paint nor glue — never design a melamine part needing a glued joint on a faced surface
- most cores are still particle board, with the edge-screw and sag limits that carries
- nearly everything is 5/8"; at 3/4" or 1" the decor choice collapses to a handful

### Plywood — the five sub-families

[View on sanmiguelmadera.com][sm-plywood]

<img src="https://www.sanmiguelmadera.com/web/image/product.template/89368/image_1024" alt="Plywood sheet, showing the face and the laminated edge" width="240">


- The subcategory that matters most for furniture.
- Five distinct products share the word "plywood". They are **not** interchangeable.
- mm figures below are the arithmetic conversion of the nominal inch size. Sheets commonly run **under** nominal — measure the actual sheet before modelling a joint to it. The caddy assumes 12 mm for a sheet sold as 1/2" (12.7 mm nominal).

**About the weight columns.** San Miguel publishes no weights, so these are calculated, not measured. Treat them as ±15%.

- Sheet area is 1.22 × 2.44 m = 2.977 m². `kg/sheet = kg/m² × 2.977`.
- Assumed densities: sangre and tratado 575 kg/m³, fenólico pino 525, black film 650, ranurado 500 (the grooves remove material).
- Black film is the heaviest per mm because formwork panels use dense cores and carry a lot of resin. Ranurado is the lightest.
- Anything over about 25 kg is a two-person lift, or a one-person lift you will regret.

#### Plywood sangre — Tropical hardwood utility plywood

- *Sangre* means "blood", for the reddish colour of the wood.
- The Central American equivalent of **lauan / meranti** utility ply — cheap red-brown sheet for drawer bottoms, backs, and painted casework.

> **Species not confirmed.** San Miguel's listing states no species. Across Central America "sangre" / "sangre real" / "palo de sangre" is a common name for **Virola koschnyi** (Myristicaceae), a light tropical hardwood of about 544 kg/m³, documented as a plywood species, works and glues well, no decay resistance. But San Miguel's Construex listing is titled "Plywood Sangre / **Madera Tropical**", which suggests the trade name describes a reddish colour class rather than one identified species. Treat it as generic mixed tropical hardwood ply.

**Typical use:**
- painted indoor furniture, carcasses, drawer boxes, cabinet backs
- shop fixtures
- the rolling cart

**Pros**
- much lighter than MDF at the same thickness — a 1/2" sheet is manageable solo
- edge screws hold, which MDF and particle board do not do reliably
- cross-banded plies resist racking, so a cart or box stays square under load
- cheapest real plywood here: 1/2" at Q199 undercuts phenolic pine at Q219.50
- widest thickness range of any panel in the shop, 1/8" through 1"

**Cons**
- the face has patches, colour variation, and coarse grain that telegraphs through paint unless filled and sanded
- core voids are normal at this grade — a void under a screw or on a cut edge leaves the joint nothing to bite into
- sheets are often bowed. Buy flat, store flat.
- not rated for exterior use; the glue line is interior grade
- face veneer is thin enough that heavy sanding breaks through

#### Plywood fenólico pino — Phenolic-bonded pine plywood

- **Fenólico describes the glue, not the face.** Phenol-formaldehyde resin, the WBP (weather- and boil-proof) adhesive used in exterior plywood.
- The face is radiata pine, either San Miguel's own or Arauco's ARAUCOPLY.

**Typical use:**
- outdoor structures, formwork
- workbench tops, anything exposed to damp
- painted furniture, if you seal the knots

**Pros**
- the glue line survives wetting — the only plywood here you can leave outside
- pine faces are lighter in colour and take primer better than sangre
- stiffer than sangre at the same thickness
- available at 1/4" (Q133), unusual for an exterior-rated panel

**Cons**
- live knots bleed resin through paint and reappear as brown rings months later — seal with shellac or a stain-blocking primer
- ARAUCOPLY costs roughly double the SM equivalent (Q580.50 vs Q337 at 3/4") and the listing does not say why
- phenolic glue leaves a dark glue line, visible on any exposed edge
- softer face than sangre, dents easily

- Phenolic-bonded plywood with a black phenolic **film** on both faces.
- Concrete formwork panel — the film is smooth, non-stick, waterproof, and releases from cured concrete.

**Typical use:**
- concrete forms
- reused as workbench tops, router table tops, jig bases

**Pros**
- the face is fully waterproof and needs no finish at all

- slick surface makes an excellent bench or outfeed top — glue and finish do not stick

**Cons**
- cutting breaks the film seal and water then enters the core — every cut edge needs paint or varnish
- nothing sticks to the face: you cannot glue to it or paint it
- heavy
- the black face is a design decision, not a neutral one

#### Plywood pino tratado — Preservative-treated pine plywood

- *Tratado* = pressure-treated with preservative against rot and insects.
- ARAUCOPLY brand only.

**Typical use:**
- ground contact or permanently outdoors
- decking substructure, exterior soffits, planters

**Pros**
- resists fungal decay and termites, which matters a great deal in Guatemala's climate
- rated for genuine long-term outdoor exposure, unlike untreated phenolic pine

**Cons**
- arrives damp from treatment and must dry before painting, or the finish lifts
- treatment chemicals corrode plain steel — you need stainless or hot-dip galvanised hardware, which adds cost
- sawdust should not be inhaled or burned. Not an indoor-project material.
- expensive: Q500 at 3/4" against Q337 for untreated SM phenolic
- often carries a green or brown tint that shows through light paint

#### Plywood pino ranurado — Grooved pine plywood (T1-11 siding)

- *Ranurado* = grooved. Parallel grooves machined into the face at regular spacing to mimic vertical board siding.
- Sold in the US as **T1-11**.

**Typical use:**
- exterior wall siding, gable ends, garden sheds, fence panels
- interior feature walls where the vertical-groove look is wanted

**Pros**
- one sheet replaces a whole run of siding boards, far faster to install
- grooves hide the fastener lines and the panel-to-panel joints
- makes a convincing slatted feature wall indoors at a fraction of the cost of real slats

**Cons**
- the grooves are a one-way commitment — you cannot fill them back to a flat panel
- grooves collect dust and are tedious to paint, needing a brush after the roller
- only two thicknesses, neither thick enough to be structural alone
- cutting across the grooves leaves a ragged exit point in each one

### Tablero de madera — Solid wood edge-glued panel

[View on sanmiguelmadera.com][sm-tablero-madera]

<img src="https://www.sanmiguelmadera.com/web/image/product.template/89309/image_1024" alt="Solid wood edge-glued panel, showing the glued strips" width="240">


- Real solid lumber ripped into strips and glued edge-to-edge into a full sheet.
- Three joint types:
  - **Listonado** — strips glued edge to edge, full length. Continuous grain.
  - **Finger joint** — short pieces joined end-to-end with interlocking fingers. Visible zigzag.
  - **But joint** — short pieces joined end-to-end with a plain square joint.
- Grade markers: **AB** is clear, **c/nudo** (*con nudo*) allows knots.

**Typical use:**
- table tops, shelves, stair treads
- drawer sides, door and frame stock
- anywhere you want solid-wood strength and edge-screw performance in sheet form

**Pros**
- real wood throughout — shape it, round it over, plane it, cut a mortise in the edge, no void or core to hit
- edge screws hold as well as in solid lumber; nothing else in this category matches that
- takes stain and clear finish the way solid wood does, because it is solid wood
- damage sands out instead of sanding through to a core

**Cons**
- by far the most expensive family here — Q699 to Q1,098 per sheet, three to four times plywood
- solid wood moves with humidity; a wide panel fixed rigidly at both ends will cup or split

## Glossary — decoding a Spanish listing

| Spanish | English | What it tells you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **tablero** | board, panel | The generic word for any sheet good. |
| **aglomerado** | particle board, chipboard | Pressed wood chips. Cheapest, weakest. |
| **enchapado** | veneered | A thin decorative wood face is glued to a cheaper core. |
| **fenólico** | phenolic | Describes the **glue**, not the face. Phenol-formaldehyde resin — waterproof, exterior rated. |
| **tratado** | pressure-treated | Impregnated with preservative against rot and termites. Use corrosion-resistant fasteners. |
| **ranurado** | grooved | Face is machined with parallel grooves. T1-11 siding profile. |
| **crudo** | raw, unfinished | No face treatment, no coating. You finish it. |
| **listonado** | edge-glued (from strips) | Solid wood strips glued edge to edge into a panel. |
| **finger joint** | finger-jointed | Short lengths joined end-to-end with interlocking fingers. Visible zigzag. |
| **but joint** | butt-jointed | Short lengths joined end-to-end with a plain square joint. |
| **c/nudo** (*con nudo*) | with knots | Knots are allowed in this grade. They bleed through paint. |
| **1C / 2C** | one face / two faces | How many sides carry the good veneer or finish. |
| **AB** | grade AB | The clear, better grade in the solid-panel range. |
| **respaldo** | backing | The thin pre-finished board used to close a cabinet back. |
| **tapacanto** | edge banding | Tape that covers a raw panel edge. |
| **cartón piedra** | hardboard | Literally "stone cardboard". Thin, dense fibreboard. |
| **perforado** | perforated | Pegboard. |
| **liso** | smooth, plain | Unpatterned, no decor printed on it. |
| **melamina** | melamine-faced board | Core with a fused decor paper on both faces. Arrives finished. |
| **hidrófugo** | moisture-resistant | The MR/green grade. **San Miguel does not stock this.** |
| **top / barra** | countertop / bar top | Post-formed laminate work surface. |
| **importada / nacional** | imported / domestic | Origin. Domestic is usually cheaper and less consistent. |

Common decor names you will meet in the melamina and respaldo lists: *blanca* (white), *negra* (black), *caoba* (mahogany), *cerezo/cherry* (cherry), *nogal* (walnut), *roble* (oak), *maple* (maple), *cedro* (cedar), *almendra* (almond), *wengué* (a very dark brown), *gris* (grey), *super mate* (super-matte finish).

## What San Miguel does **not** carry

Verified by searching their shop directly — each of these returns zero results:

- **Cabinet-grade birch plywood** (*abedul*, "Baltic birch"). No birch, no Baltic ply, no multi-ply void-free panel of any species. The closest substitutes are enchapado caoba 2C (a good face, but a standard voided core) or plywood sangre (cheap, but patched faces and voids).
- **Moisture-resistant MDF** (*hidrófugo*, MR, "green MDF"). Every MDF here is standard interior grade.
- **Fibre-cement board** (*fibrocemento*, Durock, Hardiebacker). No cement backer board at all.
- **Contrachapado** as a search term returns nothing — the shop indexes everything under the English word "plywood".
- **Okoumé panels.** Okoumé appears only as a finished door product (`PUERTA PLYWOOD OKOUME`), not as sheet stock.

Two practical consequences. First, if you need a void-free panel for exposed plywood edges — the "plywood aesthetic" with a visible striped edge — San Miguel cannot supply it, and you should plan to band or lip your edges instead. Second, there is no water-resistant option in the MDF range at all, so any MDF part that might meet water needs to be plywood instead.
