# Tools for the Caddy Build

What you need to build the caddy, assuming your supplier delivers all
parts cut to final shape (including the side-panel polygon — see
Assembly.md § The side-panel shape).

If your supplier cannot do the angled cut and you have to do it
yourself, you'll need a small additional kit — see § If you have to
cut the diagonal yourself at the bottom.

## Power tools

### Drill / driver — required

A cordless drill is fine. You use it for drilling clearance holes and
driving Confirmat screws.

Accessories (all required):

- **Confirmat stepped drill bit** (M5 / 40mm) — drills the pilot,
  clearance, and counterbore in one pass. Without this you cannot use
  Confirmat joinery.
- **3mm hex bit** — drives the M5 Confirmat heads. Much faster than an
  L-shaped Allen key for ~30 screws.

Optional accessory:

- **Drill guide / right-angle jig** — keeps the bit at 90°. Tilt is the
  #1 way to blow out a Confirmat joint in 12mm plywood.

### Pin / brad nailer — optional

A 23-gauge pin nailer or 18-gauge brad nailer tacks pieces in place
while glue cures. Saves you holding parts by hand while you find the
drill. Not essential — clamps and a steady hand also work.

## Hand tools — required

- **Awl or center punch** — dimples drill starts so the stepped bit
  does not wander.
- **Tape measure** — for laying out screw positions.
- **Pencil** (sharp 0.5mm mechanical, or HB sharpened) — mark every
  screw center.
- **Combination or framing square** — keeps the drill bit perpendicular
  and squares the base assembly before the glue sets.
- **Utility knife** — opens packaging, trims hardened glue, scores any
  filler before sanding.
- **Damp rag** — wipe glue squeeze-out before it dries.

## Hand tools — recommended

- **2–4 small quick-grip clamps** (~150–300mm jaw) — for holding pieces
  during anchor-and-pivot and clamping the lip. You do *not* need 700mm
  bar clamps.
- **Scrap-plywood registration block** (~100 × 100mm with a clean
  factory 90° corner) — your right-angle jig for solo assembly. Free;
  cut from offcuts.
- **Safety glasses** — drilling plywood edges throws chips.
- **Sanding block + sandpaper** (120, 180 grit) — eases sharp edges and
  cleans tear-out around drilled holes. Add 80 grit if you end up
  cutting the diagonal yourself.
- **Flexible putty knife / filling knife** (~25–50mm blade) — presses
  wood filler into edge voids and scrapes the excess flush.

## Hand tools — optional

- **Bench or sawhorses + a flat work surface** — assembling on the
  floor is miserable and hard to keep square. Strictly optional, but
  hugely worth it.
- **Block plane or sharp chisel** — for shaving a proud edge flush
  faster than sanding can.
- **Cabinet scraper or single-cut mill file** — flattens dried filler
  and cleans plywood edges without dishing them the way coarse paper
  can.

## Consumables

- **Wood glue** (PVA / yellow glue) — required. Every joint gets glue.
  One small bottle is enough.
- **Wood filler in a natural / light tone** — recommended. Plywood
  edges have internal voids and the occasional small hole. Since the
  plywood is staying visible, pick a filler that dries close to the ply
  color (or a clear / translucent epoxy filler). Avoid generic "dark
  wood" filler — it reads as a stain against the light edge plies.

## Not needed

- **Large bar clamps** — anchor-and-pivot makes them unnecessary.
- **Pocket-hole jig** — this build uses Confirmat, not pocket screws.
- **Router or dado blade** — no grooves or rabbets in this build.
- **Edge banding + iron** — the plywood edges stay visible by design.

## If you have to cut the diagonal yourself

Only needed if your supplier refuses the angled cut. See Assembly.md
§ Cutting the diagonal yourself for the procedure.

- **Jigsaw** — for the two short cuts per panel. A corded one is fine;
  borrow if you can before buying — €70+ for two cuts is steep.
- **Fine-tooth jigsaw blade** (Bosch T101AO downcut or similar).
  Coarser than ~10 TPI will splinter the surface ply.
- **Long straightedge** (steel rule, aluminium level, or a factory-edged
  scrap board ≥ 350mm) — clamps to the panel as a saw guide.
- **80-grit sandpaper** — add to your sanding kit to clean the cut
  down to the scored line. Block plane or chisel work too.
