# Assembly Guide

A plywood caddy built with **Confirmat screws + wood glue**. One screw type,
one glue, one method. No pocket-hole jig, no router.

If a word is unfamiliar (Confirmat, counterbore, end-grain, etc.) check the
mini-glossary at the bottom.

## Before you start

You need:

- **Tools** — see `Tools.md`.
- **Hardware** — see § Hardware below.
- **Parts** — all 8 cut per `Cutlist.md`. The two side panels are not
  pure rectangles — see § The side-panel shape for what to ask your
  supplier to do.
- **Workspace** — a flat floor or bench big enough to lay the bottom plate
  flat (~290 × 720mm) and stand a side panel next to it.

## Hardware

- **~35 Confirmat screws**, M5 × 40mm (a 50-piece kit covers it with
  spares).
- **2 wood screws**, ~25mm long, for the hidden lip attachment from below.
- **16 short wood screws** for the four caster plates (size depends on the
  caster — check the package).
- **Wood glue** (PVA / yellow glue). One small bottle is plenty.
- **Optional pin nails** (23 ga) or brads (18 ga) to tack pieces while
  glue cures.

## Five things to know first

1. **Glue every joint.** Even with a screw. Glue + screw is roughly twice
   as strong as either alone in plywood.
2. **Drill perpendicular.** Tilt is the #1 way to ruin a Confirmat joint
   in 12mm plywood — even 5° will blow out the edge. Use a square against
   the panel to check the bit, or clamp a small block as a guide.
3. **Stay ~25mm from any edge.** Plywood splits near edges. Every screw
   center should be at least 25mm from the nearest edge of the receiving
   board.
4. **Wipe glue squeeze-out immediately** with a damp rag. Dried PVA is
   hard to remove and blocks any finish from soaking in.
5. **Don't back Confirmat screws out and re-drive them.** The threads
   chew the plywood on each cycle. Dry-fit *without* glue once if you
   must; then assemble for real, screws in, done.

## The side-panel shape

The side panels are not rectangles. The top edge slopes from
`backHeight = 712mm` at the back down to `frontHeight = 625mm` at the
front, with small flats at both ends of the slope.

**Ask your supplier to cut this shape.** A 20° angled cut on a 284 ×
737mm piece is well within what a panel-saw shop can do. Don't assume
no — ask explicitly.

Spec to give them (per panel, ×2):

- **Outer rectangle:** 284 × 737mm.
- **Cut off the front-top corner** along these two lines (origin at the
  front-bottom corner; x runs back along the long edge, y runs up):
  - From `(0, 650)` to `(22, 650)` — 22mm horizontal flat.
  - From `(22, 650)` to `(262, 737)` — 240 × 87mm diagonal (~20°).

If your supplier truly cannot do the angle, see § Cutting the diagonal
yourself at the bottom of this doc.

## The build, step by step

Read the whole list before starting. Some steps are easier if you've laid
out earlier ones first.

### Mark and drill all the side-panel holes first

Doing all the drilling with the side panels flat on the bench is much
easier than drilling them once they are vertical.

1. **Mark every Confirmat position** on the *outside* face of each side
   panel, using the position tables in `Build/Screws.md` (generated by
   `task run`; see § Confirmat plan). Pencil, square, tape measure.
   Measure twice.
2. **Dimple each mark** with an awl or center punch so the stepped bit
   does not wander.
3. **Drill all clearance holes** through each side panel with the
   Confirmat stepped bit. Keep the drill perpendicular — use a square
   to check, or clamp a small block beside the bit as a guide.

After this step every side-panel hole is done. The rest is positioning,
gluing, and driving screws.

### Base sub-assembly

This is the foundation. Square *now*; you cannot fix it later.

4. Lay the **bottom plate** face-down on the bench (the side that will
   face the floor is up).
5. Stand the **cross-brace** on edge along the centerline of the bottom
   plate's underside. Glue the contact strip, hold it upright, drive
   3–4 wood screws up through the bottom plate into the brace's edge.
   *(Screws go from the bottom plate down into the brace, so the heads
   are on what will become the floor side.)*
6. Glue the **front kick plate**'s top edge and stand it on edge across
   the front of the bottom plate. Drive 2–3 wood screws down through
   the bottom plate into its top edge.
7. Repeat for the **back kick plate** at the back edge.
8. **Square the base** with a framing square at all four corners before
   the glue sets. Adjust if needed; you have ~15 minutes with PVA.
9. Let the base sit while you do the next step — the glue does not need
   to be fully cured before adding the sides, but it should be set
   enough that nothing shifts.

### Sides

10. Stand the base upright (cross-brace and kick plates pointing down).
11. **Glue the inside face of the left side panel** along the contact
    strip that will meet the bottom plate, the front kick plate end,
    and the back kick plate end.
12. Use the **registration block** technique (see § Techniques) to
    set the side panel at 90° to the bottom plate.
13. Use the **anchor-and-pivot** technique (see § Techniques) to drive
    the Confirmat screws — first one near the front, then near the back,
    then the rest.
14. Repeat for the **right side panel**.

### Back plate

15. Slide the **back plate** in from the top, between the side panels,
    resting on the back strip of the bottom plate.
16. Glue all three contact edges: bottom (to bottom plate's rear strip),
    left (to left side panel), right (to right side panel).
17. Drive the Confirmat screws through the side panels into the back
    plate's edges (already drilled in step 3).
18. Drive 2–3 wood screws up through the bottom plate's rear strip into
    the back plate's bottom edge.

### Shelves

19. **Shelf 1** first. Slide it in horizontally between the side panels
    at z = 287mm (top surface). Glue the two side edges. Drive 3
    Confirmat screws per side from outside.
20. **Shelf 2** next, at z = 600mm. Glue the two side edges. Drive 3
    Confirmat screws per side.

### Front lip

The lip closes off the front of shelf 2 and stiffens the top.

21. Glue the **back face** of the lip (the 12 × 688mm face) and press it
    against the **front edge of shelf 2**, with its bottom flush with
    shelf 2's bottom.
22. Drive **1 Confirmat per side** through each side panel into the
    lip's end.
23. From under shelf 2, drive **2 wood screws** (25mm) up into the
    lip's bottom face. Hidden once installed; gives a face-grain pull
    that the Confirmat alone cannot.

### Casters

24. Carefully **lay the cabinet upside down** on a workbench or padded
    floor. The back plate prevents racking — but support it.
25. Position the four caster plates near each corner of the bottom plate.
26. Drive the caster screws straight down into the bottom plate. Pre-drill
    pilot holes if the caster screws are small (≤4mm).
27. **Optional**: glue a triangular scrap of plywood into each inside
    corner where the side-panel foot meets the bottom plate. Doubles
    the screw-holding for the caster screws and stiffens the corner.

### Done

Stand it up. Push it. Listen for squeaks. Wipe any glue squeeze-out you
missed.

## Confirmat plan

Where the screws go. Distances are from the nearest edge of the receiving
board; centered across the board's thickness.

| Joint | Screws | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Side panel ↔ bottom plate | 3 per side (6 total) | Heaviest load path. |
| Side panel ↔ shelf 1 | 3 per side (6 total) | Tray + middle shelf load. |
| Side panel ↔ shelf 2 | 3 per side (6 total) | Symmetric with other shelves. |
| Side panel ↔ front kick plate | 1 per side (2 total) | Centered in the 25mm-tall kick plate end. |
| Side panel ↔ back kick plate | 1 per side (2 total) | Centered in the 25mm-tall kick plate end. |
| Side panel ↔ back plate | 4 per side (8 total) | Distribute top to bottom. |
| Side panel ↔ front lip | 1 per side (2 total) | Centered in the 37mm lip end. |
| Cross-brace ↔ bottom plate (from below) | 3–4 wood screws | Drive up into the bottom plate's underside. |
| Shelf 2 ↔ front lip (from below) | 2 wood screws | Hidden under shelf 2. |

~30 Confirmats + ~7 wood screws + glue everywhere.

## Techniques

Two techniques referenced from the steps above.

### Registration block

A free 90° jig. Cut a scrap of plywood ~100 × 100mm with a clean
factory corner.

To set a side panel square to the bottom plate: lay the block on the
bottom plate with its corner in the inside angle where the side panel
will sit. Press the side panel against the block. The block holds the
right angle while you drive the first screw. Remove and continue.

### Anchor-and-pivot

How to fasten a 700mm joint without giant clamps.

1. Side-panel holes are already drilled (step 3).
2. With the side panel held against the base, drive the **stepped bit**
   through one of the existing clearance holes into the base's edge.
   This pilots the receiving hole using the side panel as a template.
3. Drive the **first Confirmat** through that hole. The joint is now
   hinged at that point.
4. Pivot the other end of the side panel into alignment — only one
   degree of freedom now.
5. Drive a Confirmat at the other end.
6. Pilot and drive the remaining screws between them.

The Confirmat is aggressive enough to pull the joint tight as it
tightens — you do not need to clamp both ends at once.

## Mini-glossary

- **Confirmat** — a chunky euro-style screw whose threads grip directly
  into plywood edges. No insert needed.
- **Stepped bit** — special drill bit for Confirmat screws. Drills the
  pilot hole, clearance hole, and counterbore in one pass.
- **Pilot hole** — small hole the screw threads bite into.
- **Clearance hole** — larger hole the screw shank passes through
  without gripping.
- **Counterbore** — wider top recess that lets the screw head sit flush
  or below the surface.
- **End-grain** — the cut end of plywood plies. Holds screws less well
  than the broad face.
- **Face-grain** — the broad face of the plywood. Screws and glue hold
  best here.
- **Squeeze-out** — glue pushed out of a joint when the screw tightens.
  Wipe with a damp rag before it dries.
- **PVA** — common yellow wood glue. Polyvinyl acetate.

## Cutting the diagonal yourself

Fallback if your supplier truly cannot do the 20° cut. Needs a jigsaw +
a few extras you may not already own — try harder to get the supplier
to do it before going this route.

You need: jigsaw, fine-tooth downcut blade (e.g. Bosch T101AO), long
straightedge, utility knife, block plane *or* 80-grit sanding block,
plus the marking tools you already have.

Start from 284 × 737mm rectangular blanks. Per panel:

1. Lay the blank flat on a bench or sawhorses. Best face up.
2. Mark the three corner points `(0, 650)`, `(22, 650)`, `(262, 737)`
   with a square, a tape, and a sharp pencil.
3. Draw the two cut lines connecting them.
4. **Score** both lines with the utility knife held against a
   straightedge. Two firm passes.
5. **Clamp the long straightedge** parallel to the first cut, offset by
   the distance from your jigsaw blade to the edge of the saw's shoe.
   The shoe rides this guide.
6. **Saw the horizontal cut.** Stop *at* the inside corner `(22, 650)`.
   Do not try to round the corner with the saw.
7. Lift the saw. Re-clamp the guide for the diagonal cut.
8. **Saw the diagonal cut**, starting from the same corner. Let the saw
   feed itself — pushing causes the blade to flex and drift.
9. The waste piece falls off.
10. **Clean to the line** with the block plane or 80-grit on a sanding
    block, then 120 and 180 grit. The scored line tells you when to
    stop.

Do both panels before starting the build.
